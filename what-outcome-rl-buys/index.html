What Outcome RL Buys:
Finishing, Reliability, Rarely Reach

Shan Yang · Independent Researcher

Paper (arXiv, soon) PDF Code & data BibTeX

Outcome RL raises pass@1, and every pass@1 is scored under a token budget. We read 20 released RL models (1.5B to 32B) against the thinking models they started from, at fixed budgets, and split each change in pass@1 into three parts that add up exactly.

Finishing

Answering before the budget runs out.

Reliability

Being right once finished.

Reach

Solving problems the base answered wrongly in all eight tries, minus the reverse.

Findings

  1. Where the base often runs out of budget, most of the change is finishing: 82% at 4k and 8k tokens. Given more tokens, the base closes most of that gap by itself.
  2. Where the base has enough tokens, the gain is reliability. The RL model is graded right more often, even on problems both models finish.
  3. By our definition, RL rarely solves what its base cannot. Reach adds at most 2.1 percentage points on any of our four physics and math sets. Of 144 physics problems only the RL model solved, 3.5% hold up when resampled or read. In a random sample, nine of ten new RL steps a human confirmed also appear in the base's samples.
  4. In a small-scale RL run on an 8B model, the gain on new problems is finishing, with reliability flat. Supplying each problem's physical setup was not shown to improve on plain RL.

The split at each recipe's own cap

Every recipe also trains under a budget, so we read each one at its own training cap and at the next budget above it. Shares are each part's size over the three parts' total, pooled over the 20 recipes on physics. Brackets are 95% intervals from resampling the seven bases.

What is released

The repository holds everything needed to recompute the paper's measurements from row-level data, without a GPU.

MetricsThe exact three-part split, the mirror control, cap selection, per-cell problem-clustered intervals, crossed and base-resampled bootstraps, and the frozen answer matcher with the judge protocol that audits its misses.
Test setsIds and gold answers for 300 physics problems, 671 harder unfiltered physics problems, 276 competition-math problems (2025 contests, plus AIME and HMMT February 2026) and MATH-500. Problem texts come from their original sources through a builder script that checks every text against its hash.
RowsOne line per sample (problem, sample index, finished, score, length) for every model and budget in the main study. Tighter budgets are derived from one generation per model.
Paper tablesEvery table as CSV, the scripts that wrote them, and tests that rebuild the cap split, the per-cell intervals and the pooled shares from the rows.
